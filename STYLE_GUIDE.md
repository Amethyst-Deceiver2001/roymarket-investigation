# Style guide — РОЙ.МАРКЕТ / ЦБСТ investigation

Standing rules for every word and every page this project publishes: the
narrative recap, the dossier, the company profiles, the methodology and
provenance pages, captions, and both language versions of all of them.

Adopted 2026-08-22, at the start of the rebuild of `docs/index.html` into a
generated multi-page publication.

**Where this file lives, and why not in `docs/`.** `docs/` becomes the published
site. This is an internal working document — it contains an analysis of the
author's own voice, which is a tool for drafting, not something the audience
needs. Keep it at the repo root. (The presentation half of it derives from a
guide that *is* published in the sibling `mariupol-property-seizures` project;
the voice half was deliberately kept private there. That split is preserved
here.)

**Read before drafting anything.** A draft in generic register is not a draft
that needs editing — it needs rewriting, which wastes the whole pass.

---

## Part I — Voice

The prose is written in the voice of **Alexey Kovalev** (independent journalist,
ex-Meduza investigations editor; Foreign Policy, NYT, Guardian, openDemocracy;
OSINT and expert-witness consultant for The Docket and The Reckoning Project).
He is a professional writer and judges generic AI prose harshly. Matching the
voice is what makes a draft usable.

### 1.1 Mode selection — which register for which page

Do not write everything in one voice. Pick the mode from what the section is
*doing*.

| Page / section | Mode | Why |
|---|---|---|
| `index` narrative recap | **Investigative** | It reports findings, not an argument |
| Company profiles | **Expert-witness / forensic** | Must survive a hostile lawyer |
| `methodology`, `sources`, "what is not established" | **Expert-witness / forensic**, flattest register | Every sentence quotable out of context without losing accuracy |
| "Why this matters" / afterword / synthesis | **FP-analysis** | It argues a thesis |
| Closing call to action, if there is one | **Campaigning**, sparingly | One barbed aside at most |
| Anything at all | **Never academic mode** | It is the control case for suppressed voice |

### 1.2 Shared DNA — applies in every mode, both languages

- **Euphemism autopsy.** Take the perpetrator's sanitising word and dissect it.
  In EN: italicise and gloss. In RU: «scare-quote» and unpack. Never let the
  regime's terminology stand unmarked. See §5.1 for this project's specific list.
- **Self-incrimination as method.** Let their own documents convict them.
  Editorialise by juxtaposition, not by adjective.
- **Named specificity. Hedge and source every number.** "at least 145,000",
  "(a figure exceeding even the highest independent assessments)".
- **"Not an isolated incident but a policy"** reframe; historical anchoring.
- **Rule of three.**
- **Hard aphoristic kicker** to close an argument — one turned sentence,
  antithesis or callback. (Not for findings pieces — see 1.3.)

### 1.3 Investigative mode — the recap and case write-ups

- **Headline is the finding, stated flat.** No hedge in the head; earn it after.
- **Deadpan accumulation over commentary.** Put two facts side by side and stop.
  Let the gap work. **Do not explain the irony.**
- **Officials get rope, not adjectives.** Quote the evasive non-answer verbatim
  and let it stand as the characterisation.
- **Bureaucratic and technical detail is evidence, not texture** — contract sums,
  ОКВЭД codes, frequencies, headcounts exist to prove systematicity.
- **Methodology-first debunking.** Don't argue a claim is wrong; go find what is
  actually behind its numbers and let it collapse on its own.
- **Anticlimactic, non-resolving endings are valid.** A findings piece can just
  stop. The system absorbing exposure and continuing *is* the point. Use this
  instead of a kicker when the piece reports rather than argues.

### 1.4 Expert-witness / forensic mode — profiles, methodology, provenance

The most important mode in this project. It is the register the evidentiary
backbone must be written in.

- **"Officially X, but actually Y" is the organising unit**, repeated for every
  entity and every actor. In this investigation that maps directly onto the
  ОКВЭД-mismatch pattern: a drone manufacturer registered as a provider of
  professional training; a naval-drone yard registered for *sport* shipbuilding.
  Make it structural, not an aside.
- **Epistemic hedging calibrated to evidentiary weight.** "it is not clear
  whether", "likely", "it is equally possible that". Never let suspicion read as
  established fact.
- **Every claim sourced to a named origin.** Nothing floats as bare assertion.
- **Chain-of-command and ownership mapping is the actual content** — who owns
  whom, who appoints whom, whose money arrives where.
- **Self-incrimination tracked temporally.** Dating precisely when a claim
  *preceded* the thing it later explained is a verification technique, not a
  rhetorical one.
- **Zero flourish, zero humour, no kicker.** Flatter than academic prose.

### 1.5 FP-analysis mode — synthesis and afterword

Cold-open on one named human (name, age, place, date), then zoom to the system.
Thesis as contrarian inversion by paragraph two or three. Appositive
identification of every actor — title, and subscriber count where it is a
channel. Concrete physical metaphor only. Generous credit to other journalists by
name. First person rare, and only evidentiary. Shorter, clipped sentences.

### 1.6 Campaigning mode — use sparingly, and only where marked

Cold open with direct intimate address to the reader. Sarcasm as connective
tissue, but always sitting *next to* a document citation, never replacing one.
Numbers as punchlines — set up the official figure, drop the real one after.
Register-mixing: registry codes in the same paragraph as slang. Sentence-rhythm
extremes. Breaks the fourth wall at the close.

In this project: at most a closing call to action, or one aside where an
official's claim is absurd enough to deserve a jab instead of silent
juxtaposition.

### 1.7 EN and RU are written separately

**RU pages are written, never translated from the EN.** The registers are
genuinely different, and a translated RU page reads as translationese.

**RU register:** long periodic sentences built on colon-then-elaboration and
chained тире — do **not** chop them into short sentences. Тире carries the main
beat. Anaphora for the climax. **First person «я»/«мы» is welcome** where EN
suppresses it. **Direct address to the reader** is available («Попробуйте
представить»). Native idiom — «без обиняков», «хотя бы видимость», «хотелось бы
надеяться». The engine is the collision of dry bureaucratic-legal lexis
(«отчуждение», «учредитель», «уставный капитал», «ОКВЭД 26.11») against visceral
moral nouns.

### 1.7a How Kovalev actually writes in Russian — read from his texts

Added 2026-10-23 at user direction, from a reading of his own published Russian

his; the quotations below are verbatim):

  Su-24 shoot-down, Cologne, the Brexit referendum and its aftermath, the Turkish

  carnegieendowment.org, which serves the carnegie.ru archive. This is the model
  for the forensic and methodology register; see "Dry analytical register" below.
  With it, openDemocracy Russia, «Жизнь после фактов: как российские

  the Wayback capture of 11.09.2019 (the live page is behind a sign-in wall). Its
  pull quotes and photo captions are the editors', not his.
- **Self-published investigation:** Лапшеснималочная on Medium, «Кто снимает
  пропагандистские ролики для московской мэрии…» (03.08.2018) and «Культ личности
  Сергея Собянина, кто его обслуживает (и кто за это платит)» (06.09.2017,
  co-written with Соня Соколова, Дмитрий Хомак and Иван Ёжиков; its block

- **Edited investigation:** Meduza, «Откуда взялся „компромат“ про связи Трампа с
  Россией?…» (03.11.2020; Meduza house style, so ё is stripped and the voice is
  flattened by the desk).
- **Profiles of named perpetrators:** pogony.org dossiers on commanders, 2026
  (dossier 194, Рамиль Ульмасбаев; dossier 190, военком Андрей Сурков). The
  closest analogue to this project's company and person profiles.
- **Columns and channel posts:** six opinion pieces on echofm.online (2024–2026)
  and posts on his Telegram channel `noodleremoverplus`; the Mariupol project
  description (2025), quoted by Медиазона.

**Structural habits (use in investigative and forensic RU).**

- **The subject's own words as the epigraph, then the record against them.**
  «Одна фраза из этого текста станет эпиграфом ко всей его дальнейшей истории: …
  В реальности, как вы наверняка догадываетесь, дело обстояло ровно наоборот.» The
  quote comes first and is left whole; the documents do the contradicting.
- **«Формально X. А Y»** is how "officially X, actually Y" sounds in his Russian:
  «Формально Рамиль Ульмасбаев не был даже основателем отряда… А Ульмасбаев
  принял командование уже готовым…». For this project: «Формально — „обучение
  специалистов“ по ОКВЭД. А на деле…».
- **Self-contradiction tracked over time** is a verification step, stated by
  date: two interviews two years apart with different wars listed; medals for
  1990 set against the man's age. Give both dates and let the gap stand.
- **Promises listed, then one flat verdict, then the counterexamples.** A long
  sentence lists what the official promised; the next is «Это всё оказалось
  враньём.»; then «Оказалось, например, что…» with concrete cases. The denial,
  then the reversal days later («Всего через несколько дней Сурков сделал крутой
  разворот…»), is the same move.
- **The individual, then the system.** «История „Ватана“ — это типичный продукт
  региональной системы…», followed at once by numbers with a denominator and a
  named source: «около 248 на 100 тысяч жителей, почти в 9 раз больше, чем по
  Москве», «в базе „Медиазоны“». A per-capita or comparative figure, never a bare
  total.
- **Dossier shape** (pogony): a list of findings in the past tense, each one
  verb-led («Организовал…», «Приказал…», «Выстроил…»); then a dated chronology
  with the exact article and court («ч. 1 ст. 112 УК РФ… прекращено за истечением
  сроков давности (Кировский районный суд Уфы)»); then the narrative. Our RU
  profiles can follow the same order.
- **Named credit, with an appositive:** «репортёр Vice Саймон Островский (автор
  одного из лучших расследований про ранний, ихтамнетный этап вторжения)»;
  «Медиазона», «Пруфы», «Ищи своих» named as the outlets they are.
- **Hedging, Meduza desk style** (the forensic RU register): «похоже»,
  «по-видимому», «судя по всему», «впрочем», «якобы», and the bracketed caveat
  «(Коэн это отрицал; последующее расследование… не нашло подтверждений…)». The
  hedge is placed on the claim it qualifies, not at the head of the paragraph.

**Dry analytical register (Carnegie): an analysis, and the template for forensic RU.**

What makes these columns his is not a formatting convention. It is how they
reason. Carnegie's house layout (the standfirst, its dash, its compelled legal
tags) is the publisher's, and none of it carries over.

*The stance: audit the consensus, his own side's included.* Every column takes a
view held by almost everyone (Trump is Putin's agent; the World Cup will be a
disaster; lying to beat Russia is justified once) and checks it against the record
with the same severity he applies to the Kremlin. The strongest pieces turn on
anti-Kremlin hawks who have dropped their standards of evidence, and on the irony
that the consensus helps its own target. For this project that is the posture
toward our own findings: a claim we would like to be true is checked hardest,
because the hostile lawyer will start there.

*The architecture: claim, its supports, the test, what is left.* He sets out the
consensus fairly, lists the arguments that hold it up, takes each against what is
actually documented, and states plainly what survives, often very little ("only
indirect evidence, and here it is"). Before judging, he separates ideas the
coverage blurs (interference from collusion), because the verdict differs for
each. The opposing case is given in its strongest form and from its best
advocates. A forensic RU profile can follow the same order: what is asserted
about the company, by whom, what each assertion rests on, what stands.

*Others speak, and he judges in the joints.* Most sentences report what a named
writer, outlet or official says, each placed by role and record and by whatever
bears on how far to trust them (a lobbyist's client list, a paper's past
fabrication). His own judgement sits in short evaluative phrases between the
reported claims, rarely in a sentence of its own. That is the model for how
our RU pages cite: the source is characterised as a source before its claim is
used.

*Measurement over adjectives.* He measures documents and claims rather than
describing them: how long a report is, which part of it is public, how much of
it is about something else, how a percentage was arrived at. Qualifiers such as
"at least" or "about" belong to the number. When he is wrong-footed by his own
first phrasing he narrows it in mid-sentence instead of hedging the whole
paragraph. The calibration words are few and used exactly: "allegedly" for an
unverified claim, "apparently" for an inference, "obviously" only where the
inference is safe.

*Irony by understatement only.* There is no sarcasm, no insult and no
intensifier. The one permitted turn is litotes: saying a thing is "not entirely"
so, or calling a leap of logic a "creative assumption". It works because
everything around it is level. In forensic mode even this goes.

*Anchoring for the Russian reader.* Foreign events are made legible through
Russian reference points (a British surveillance bill set beside the Yarovaya
package, a period of the Duma, a memorable Russian propaganda sheet of 1996) and
through historical precedents picked for scale, not decoration. Foreign terms
are kept and explained, with their history when it matters. On our RU pages the
same move explains a Western sanctions regime or a procurement norm in terms a
Russian reader already knows.

*Rhythm.* Long hypotactic sentences, with the qualification held inside them,
carry the facts. A short sentence marks a pivot. A run of questions frames a
problem before he answers it. "On the one hand / on the other" and "in words /
in deeds" pairings carry contrast without comment. The only first person is the
editorial "we".

*Endings.* One measured sentence that settles what the evidence supports, at
most with a dry turn, or a quiet close on a small human detail that the large
story missed. Never a call to action. This is §1.3's anticlimactic ending,
in its Russian form.

*What the openDemocracy essay adds.* It works the same way at essay length, and
shows five more habits:

- *The instrument goes with the figure.* A poll number comes with the
  pollster, the date, the question in its exact wording and the series over
  years, so the reader sees the change rather than a single figure. For us, a
  revenue or headcount figure comes with the filing and the year it reports
  on, and with the earlier years where we have them.
- *A figure is made to yield its consequence.* From "no more than one per cent
  read foreign media" he infers, with a plain "that is", that almost everyone
  who distrusts foreign media knows it only second-hand. The analysis lies in
  that step, not in the number.
- *Two parties compared on the same scale before they are told apart.* Age,
  reach, state awards and seats on official councils are set side by side,
  and only then the one difference that matters. The method suits two
  companies in one ownership chain, or a maker and its seller.
- *A received view stated, then tested, then answered at the close.* The
  opening states what people usually believe about the subject (state media
  as a single monolith). The body tests it, and the last sentence answers the
  opening directly. The conclusion closes the frame rather than adding a
  flourish.
- *The author's own stake is disclosed in the text, in the third person.* His
  former editorship of the outlet he cites is declared in brackets at the
  point of use, and personal experience appears as "the author's experience",
  not as "I". In this project that is the register for saying what we
  ourselves did: which pull, which request, which archive.

Two smaller points. A coarse insider word is allowed only inside quotation
marks, as the milieu's own term, and glossed at once. A claim of Russian
exceptionalism is refused: where the research shows a general human or global
pattern, he says so and names the studies, keeping each English term with its
gloss.

**Disclosed method (Лапшеснималочная): how a finding is shown to be checked.**

- **The reader is told how the fact was confirmed, and how many sources stand
  behind it:** «Я попросил его предоставить доказательства… но даже это меня не
  удовлетворило, поэтому я нашёл ещё один источник, независимый от первого,
  который информацию подтвердил, а потом и третий.» In our pages that is one
  sentence saying which document a fact rests on and what corroborates it.
- **The denial, then the register record that answers it:** «Сам Фадеев в
  разговоре со мной наотрез отрицает… Верится в это с трудом, потому что… А одним
  из совладельцев одноимённого ООО был кто? Правильно, Данила Поперечный». The
  company record does the arguing (the rhetorical question is for campaigning
  mode; the order denial → record is for every mode).
- **The non-answer is recorded as a fact:** «Все наши письма… и звонки остались
  без ответа.»
- **What was not established is said outright:** «…сколько себе оставляет
  Поперечный — это нам, к сожалению, выяснить так и не удалось, как и конечного
  заказчика.» A conclusion that depends on sources is stated conditionally: «Если
  всё обстоит так, как нам сообщают наши многочисленные источники, то…».
- **The scale of money, given a denominator the reader knows:** 200 visitors a
  day against 150 000 residents is «чуть больше 0,1% жителей района»; 19 billion
  roubles a year «столько же, сколько вся международная корреспондентская сеть…
  Russia Today»; «В России есть субъекты федерации, у которых бюджет меньше.» The
  same goes for a contract sum or a company's revenue on our pages.
- **The ownership chain through named people and their former posts:** «Александр
  Латышев, директор ООО „Единая Редакция“… — бывший пресс-секретарь префекта
  СВАО»; «Куда ни ткнись — везде одна и та же картина.» «Система устроена таким
  образом, чтобы единственным выгодополучателем в ней была сама мэрия» is the
  thesis sentence of a procurement finding.

**Sentence and word level.**

- Long sentences carry the facts; the **short sentence is the verdict** and comes
  right after one: «Это всё оказалось враньём.» «Всё равно за это ничего не
  будет.» One per paragraph at most.
- **Scare quotes do the editorialising**, word by word: «добровольческом»
  отряде, «провинившегося» бойца, «рейды», «подвигах», «региональная
  особенность». This is the RU form of the euphemism autopsy (§1.2, §5.1); it
  replaces an adjective.
- **Register collision is lexical, not only bureaucratic-vs-moral.** An article
  number sits in the same paragraph as plain speech: «на гражданке», «грести всех
  подряд», «тереть через пару часов», «закатывали в асфальт». The colloquial word
  is precise and Russian, never English slang transliterated.
- **Reader address is light and conversational**: «как вы наверняка
  догадываетесь», «Представляете…», «Подумайте», «посмотрите». Not the
  pathos-heavy «Попробуйте представить» every time.
- **Metaphor is concrete or from a shared culture** (the guide's EN rule holds):
  «как демоны Варпа», «лавкрафтианской жути», «хтоническим мраком». Use in
  columns only; never in a profile.
- **ё is written** in his own texts («подчинённых», «всё», «посёлка»); §2.5 holds.
- **Тире, not hyphen.** His fast texts slip into « - » as a dash («перевалило за 5
  тысяч - больше половины всех боевых потерь»). Our pages always use «—» with spaces.

**What stays in his columns and never reaches this project's pages.**

The Echo and Telegram register — мат («Н А Е Б А Л А»), CAPS for mocked
officialese («СПЕЦИАЛЬНАЯ ВОЕННАЯ ОПЕРАЦИЯ», «ГОСУДАРСТВЕННЫЕ ДЕНЬГИ»), mock
phonetic spellings («эсвэо», «Россиюшка», lower-case «гирькина»), «ХОБА», emoji,
the ironic inner monologue given to the subject («Главное — верить в себя!») and
insults as characterisation («лузер лузером», «садистов», «z-гниды») — is his
campaigning voice at full strength. On our RU pages:

- **Forensic mode (profiles, methodology):** none of it. The pogony profiles name
  individual killers on court records; our profiles name companies and their
  owners on register records, and must read to a court as findings, not
  contempt. Write it in the Carnegie register above, with the structural habits,
  the disclosed-method sentences and the Meduza hedges, with no characterising
  adjective and no understatement jokes.
- **Investigative mode:** the structural habits, the scare quotes, the one short
  verdict sentence, light reader address. No insults, no CAPS, no мат.
- **Campaigning mode:** one ironic turn is allowed (an official's promise set
  against the outcome, a quoted boast left to hang), still beside a document
  citation (§1.6). Never мат, CAPS or mock spellings.

A sample of the target investigative RU register, from the Mariupol project
description: «Преступления России в Мариуполе не прекратились вместе с ковровыми
обстрелами густонаселённых жилых кварталов. Просто теперь это другие военные
преступления — куда менее заметные и сильно растянутые во времени. Урбицид,
убийство города, можно совершать и без бомбардировщиков и батарей залпового
огня.» Note the shape: a negated expectation, «Просто теперь…» with the тире
beat, a term glossed in apposition («Урбицид, убийство города»), and a concrete
close.

### 1.8 Avoid, in every mode

Cheap intensifiers — *horrific*, *shocking*, *brutal*, *chilling*. Unsourced
numbers. Abstract or decorative metaphor. Choppy translationese in RU.
Explaining the irony. Hedging that reads as padding rather than as calibration.

## Part II — Language and naming

### 2.1 The core rule: English is the language of analysis, Russian is the language of evidence

Revised 2026-10-23 at user direction: the earlier rule kept every proper name in
Cyrillic on EN pages, which only readers of Russian could use. EN pages now lead
with Latin script for every name; the Cyrillic original stays one tap away or on the
line beneath, because it is the string a screener pastes into ЕГРЮЛ or a sanctions list.

- **English**: headings, narrative, argument, table column headers, `alt` text,
  captions, glosses, and the visible form of every name (§2.2–2.3a).
- **Russian, under RU** (revised 2026-10-23 at user direction: "all in English first, Russian in a pop-up"):
  every quotation (§3.1), the wording a post uses for a recipient, a line printed on a document or a picture, a
  register address, registry terminology. The page shows our English; the verbatim Russian sits behind a small
  **RU** control, copyable, in the same place every time. A quotation is marked **Translation** and set in
  quotation marks; the popover holds the original, which stays the record. Two things stay visible in Russian
  because the reader looks them up: the **registered name of a company or person beside its Latin form on a
  card**, and **bibliography titles** (cited documents and pages, as published).
- **The quotation block**: `quote_html()` in `display_helpers.py`: label, English in quotation marks, RU control,
  then the speaker and date line with its footnote. One helper, so every page does it the same way. Printed text in
  a picture caption uses `caption_html()`, a line quoted from a document `orig_line()`, and an inline Russian
  original beside a Latin form is turned into an RU control by `popup_originals()` as the page is written.
  Prose that still carries Cyrillic names goes through `latinize_gloss()`.

**In the RU pages this split collapses.** The parenthetical English glosses
disappear, the transliteration layer (§2.2) disappears entirely, and quotations
simply sit in the prose.

### 2.2 Transliteration — EN pages only

Latin script is the visible text for every proper name; the Cyrillic original is
attached, never dropped. One helper renders it (`display_helpers.name_html()`,
`person_names()`, `latin_text()`), so no page decides on its own.

- **Personal names**: conventional English order, given name first —
  "Vadim Tsyganov", not "Tsyganov V.B.".
  - **First mention**: the Latin name with the **full Cyrillic ФИО**, patronymic
    included, in brackets: "Vadim Tsyganov (Цыганов Вадим Борисович)". The patronymic
    adds nothing for a reader except telling near-namesakes apart, so it appears here
    and on cards only.
  - **After that**: surname only — "Tsyganov".
  - **Cards and evidence packets**: the full ФИО stays visible on the line beneath the
    Latin name, because they are where namesakes are told apart.
  - **Spelling**: a Latin form a sanctions list or the person's own documents use
    (`name_latin`, with its source) wins; otherwise the house transliteration
    (BGN/PCGN without diacritics, §2.5 for ё).
- **Ukrainian places**: Ukrainian-based English spelling (Zaporizhzhia, Luhansk,
  Kharkiv, Donetsk), with no popover. A Russian form appears only inside a quote.
- **Russian places**: the conventional English name where one exists (Moscow,
  Kursk Oblast, Belgorod), otherwise transliterated. Places with a conventional form
  are listed in `data/registry/name_translations.json`.
- **Brands, products, events**: transliterated, with the Cyrillic beneath on a card
  or in a popover in prose: «Bulat» («Булат»), Dronnitsa 2025, ROY.MARKET. Acronyms
  of organisations are transliterated too (TsBST, not ЦБСТ, in English prose).
- **The same person must not appear in Cyrillic in one section and Latin in
  another.** The registry holds both forms and the helper picks.
- **Never fabricate** a given name or patronymic to complete a form. "Surname
  I.O." is an honest fallback where the source gives no more.
- **Running text uses a popover, not `title=`**: `title=` does not work on touch
  screens, is unreliable in screen readers, and cannot be copied. The popover shows
  the Cyrillic form (and, for a state body, its transliteration) with a copy button.

### 2.5 ё and е — one policy for spelling, one for matching

Decided 2026-09-19. Proper names are spelled with **ё** wherever a source for that
person or entity gives it, as a house style, in Russian and in transliteration.
Matching never depends on it.

- **Store and display ё where a source has it.** «Алёшин», «Артём», «Киселёв», «Пётр»,
  «Махнёв». One source giving ё is enough; the ё form is the canonical `fio_ru`/`name_ru`.
- **Never infer ё from a name's shape, and never read a missing ё as evidence.** ЕГРЮЛ,
  passport scans and most court filings drop it. Королёв and Королев are different
  surnames, Фёдоров and Федоров both exist; add a ё only from a source for *that* person.
  Do not bulk-convert.
- **Never touch verbatim text.** Quotes, source files, `source_id`-backed strings,
  ЕГРЮЛ-derived history values and any ALL-CAPS register text keep the source's
  spelling (§3.1). The house style applies to our own prose and to the canonical name
  fields only.
- **All matching folds ё to е on both sides.** The graph builder, `check_registry.py`,
  `extract_registry.py` and `scripts/registry_lookup.py` do this; new code that compares
  names must too. In Latin keys `yo` folds to `e` as well (Kiselyov = Kiselev,
  Artyom = Artem).
- **Latin display**: render ё as **yo** ("Alyoshin", "Artyom") when we transliterate
  ourselves and no romanisation is established -- an accepted romanisation that puts е
  for ё ("Makhnev", "Kiselev") is kept, and wins over a mechanical "yo" (decided
  2026-09-19 when the duplicate `makhnyov-` record was retired); where a source gives a Latin spelling (a sanctions-list entry, a
  patent record) keep that spelling as-is, because that is the string a screener will
  search. Slugs stay ASCII and are not renamed when a ё is added.
- **Audit**: `python3 scripts/yo_audit.py` lists same-person and cross-record spelling
  splits and word-level leads. Only a same-person hit is safe to fix without a source.

### 2.3 Legal-entity names — transliterate the legal form, never translate it

**The legal-form abbreviation is part of the registered name and stays
transliterated everywhere — OOO, AO, ZAO, PAO — never rendered as LLC/JSC/CJSC.**
This is not a style preference; it is what makes the text useful for its stated
purpose. OFAC's SDN list, EU sanctions annexes, and OCCRP/Bellingcat all render
Russian entities this way, because a screening analyst matching this document
against ЕГРЮЛ needs the string that actually appears in the registry. "LLC
«Tekhnodron»" matches nothing in a company-registry search; "OOO «Tekhnodron»"
does. Translating also implies a false equivalence — "ООО" and "LLC" are not the
same liability regime, and a hostile lawyer can use that gap.

**Which form each page tier takes:**

- **Cards (chain views, profiles, packets)**: the documented brand or the
  transliterated legal name on top (`3mx`, then `OOO «Trimiks»`), the registered
  Cyrillic name on the line beneath (`ООО «Тримикс»`). A sole trader leads with the
  person's name: "Ruslan Kuznetsov", then "Sole trader (IP)", then the ЕГРИП name
  (`ИП Кузнецов Руслан Николаевич`).
- **Running text**: the brand or transliterated legal name, with the Cyrillic in a
  popover on first mention (§2.2), never as an inline prose translation.
- **Narrative recap page** (general audience): a single functional gloss in
  parentheses on the *very first* mention of the pattern in the whole document —
  "a Russian LLC (OOO)" — is permitted once, to orient a lay reader, then never
  repeated. Every individual company name after that still uses the
  transliterated form (`OOO «Tekhnodron»`), not a translated one.

| Abbreviation | Transliterated form (always) | Functional meaning (tooltip only) |
|---|---|---|
| ООО | OOO | limited liability company |
| АО | AO | joint-stock company |
| ПАО | PAO | public joint-stock company |
| ЗАО | ZAO | closed joint-stock company (pre-2015 form) |
| АНО | ANO | autonomous non-profit organisation |
| Фонд | Fond | foundation |
| ИП | IP | sole proprietor |
| НПЦ | NPTs | research and production centre |
| НПО / НПП | NPO / NPP | research and production association / enterprise |
| КБ | KB | design bureau |
| ГК | GK | group of companies |
| ФГУП | FGUP | federal state unitary enterprise |

The proper name inside «» is transliterated the same way — never translated,
never left in bare Cyrillic in EN prose.

### 2.3a State bodies — translate the name, attach the official form

Added 2026-10-23 at user direction. A ministry, directorate, administration, court
or agency has a descriptive name, so transliterating it helps no reader. It is
**translated**, with the official Cyrillic name and its sanctions-style
transliteration attached, following the "a.k.a." convention OFAC and the EU use.

- **Reader pages**: the English name is visible — "Main Directorate of the
  Emergencies Ministry (MChS) for Kursk Oblast". On a card the official name and its
  transliteration sit on the two lines beneath; in running text a popover holds both,
  each copyable, on first mention only.
- **Evidence packets and exports**: all three inline, the way list compilers write
  them — "Main Directorate of the Emergencies Ministry (MChS) for Kursk Oblast (a.k.a.
  *Glavnoe upravlenie MChS Rossii po Kurskoy oblasti*; ГЛАВНОЕ УПРАВЛЕНИЕ МЧС РОССИИ
  ПО КУРСКОЙ ОБЛАСТИ)".
- **Where the English comes from**: `data/registry/name_translations.json`, keyed by
  the official name, hand-maintained. It is presentation, not a fact. A body with no
  entry falls back to transliteration, and the page builder lists it so the gap is
  visible. Parties are translated the same way (United Russia).
- **Companies are not state bodies**, even when the state owns them: an AO or FGUP
  keeps its transliterated legal form under §2.3.

### 2.4 Identifiers

- ИНН and ОГРН are always given as bare digits with the label. Never reformatted
  with spaces or separators — they must be copy-pasteable into a registry search.
- **EN pages use the Latin labels** (revised 23.10.2026 at user direction):
  `INN 9704241986`, `OGRN 1237700218612`, as the EU and UK sanctions lists write
  them. Never "TIN" or "tax number", which find nothing. Register names in source
  titles follow: EGRUL, EGRIP, GUR. RU pages keep ИНН and ОГРН.
- On first mention of an entity in the dossier or its profile, give ИНН and ОГРН
  together. In the narrative recap, give neither unless the number is itself the
  point — the recap links to the profile.
- Dates of birth (and other person-identifying dates — passport issue date,
  registration date when quoted in prose) are rendered in Russian convention,
  `DD.MM.YYYY` (e.g. `13.04.1975`), in any published or hand-maintained page —
  profiles, the dossier, `notes/*.md` tables. `data/registry/*.json` itself keeps
  ISO `YYYY-MM-DD` for sorting/computation; the DD.MM.YYYY rule is a display-layer
  conversion applied at render/write time, not a change to the stored value.

## Part III — Evidence and sourcing

### 3.1 Quotes: never paraphrase, never invent

Any quoted material is the original text only — verbatim, punctuation and typos
preserved. A quote that cannot be verified against a primary source does not get
used.

- Draft quotes **from the original Russian**, never back-translated from the EN
  page's own prose. A single added or dropped word turns evidence into a misquote.
- **English first, original under RU** (2026-10-23): the page leads with our translation, labelled
  *Translation*; the verbatim Russian is the popover and the string a lawyer checks. The translation is ours and is
  never presented as the speaker's words in English. The original is never edited to suit the translation.
- Preserve a grammatical quirk or apparent typo and mark it `[так в оригинале]`
  rather than silently fixing it. The exception: an obvious ASR mistranscription
  in a transcript may be corrected silently — fidelity is owed to the speaker,
  not to the transcription tool.
- Never attribute someone's paraphrase of an unseen document to that document's
  author. Quote the paraphraser's own words and attribute them to the paraphraser.
- Never assert a real name behind a channel handle unless it is sourced.
- Quotes live in `data/registry/quotes.json` so the EN and RU pages render the
  same string rather than two hand-copied ones that drift.
- **Direct quotation** attributed to a speaker or document is wrapped in `<em>`.
  **Scare-quotes** marking a euphemism in our own prose stay plain «» and are not
  italicised. A document's own *title* in guillemets stays plain.

### 3.2 The "own" marker

The brief uses "its **own** filing", "ЦБСТ's **own** channel", "Градислава's
**own** accounts" throughout, to separate a self-incriminating primary source
from a third-party claim. This is self-incrimination-as-method working correctly
at sentence level. **Keep it.** Do not smooth it away as repetition.

### 3.3 Confidence vocabulary

Every sourced claim carries one of three levels, inherited from the existing
brief's tag classes and stored in the registry:

| Level | Means |
|---|---|
| `doc` | Backed by a primary document — EGRUL, БФО, Устав, court filing, the subject's own post |
| `press` | Reported by a named outlet, not independently verified here |
| `open` | Asserted, plausible, or partially evidenced — not established |

Render as a visible chip. This vocabulary is not internal notation; a reader
running a sanctions submission needs it. But **never write the class name
(`t-doc`) in prose** — see §3.5.

### 3.4 Where sourcing goes

No hashes, file paths or capture dates in narrative prose or captions. State once
that every artefact is sourced from the original and hashed, and put the full
chain-of-custody catalogue — hash, source URL, capture date — on the provenance
page, framed for readers with a professional or legal interest in independent
verification.

**No research-process dates in prose.** Drop "found 21.08.2026", "pulled
directly", "read on". A date stays only if it is a fact the narrative needs — a
filing's own date, a post's publication date, a deadline named in the source.
Test: would a reader who knows nothing about how this was researched need this
date to understand the claim?

Link to the original wherever a URL exists — `t.me/<channel>/<id>`, a filing PDF,
a press page — with short visible link text, never a bare URL in body text. If no
public URL exists, say so rather than silently linking nothing.

### 3.5 No internal notation

Never surface database column names, script names, registry field names, tag
class names, or internal jargon in published prose. `mine_leads.py`,
`source_id`, `t-doc`, `wb_overlap`, `tier: adjacent` — all of these belong in the
registry and in this repo's markdown, never on a page.

### 3.6 Trace before correcting

Before flagging any existing claim as mistaken or unconfirmed, trace it to its
origin — `grep` the scraped corpus and the `wb_watch` history for the specific
distinguishing terms together, not one of them. A plausible alternative
explanation is not the same as finding the claim's source. Declare something
mistaken only after an actual source search comes up empty or contradictory.

## Part IV — People

### 4.1 Identifying detail

Full identifying detail is published for network principals — see the PII section
of `CLAUDE.md`, which is authoritative and was rewritten 2026-08-22. Summary:
name, ФИО, DOB, birthplace, address and personal ИНН are in scope for PEPs,
principals, owners, directors and comparable public actors, because the output
feeds sanctions screening. Every such field carries its own source or is left
null. People who are not principals are **not** in scope — ask first.

### 4.2 Photographs

Sourced from the subject's public or professional presence: their own channel,
their company's site, press coverage, an official event. Not private or family
imagery. Every photograph carries its source and date in the caption data.

### 4.3 Depth is bounded by significance

Spend real effort on the figures a reader will actually focus on — the
supervisory board, the manufacturer principals, the money layer. Do not burn
effort chasing a patronymic for every name in a long roster. An honest
initials-only entry beats a guessed one.

## Part V — Project-specific standing rules

### 5.1 Euphemism list — never let these stand unmarked

| Term | What it actually denotes |
|---|---|
| «резидент» | A supplier admitted to a state-convened defence-procurement network — the word borrows the innocuous vocabulary of technology parks |
| «гуманитарная миссия» / «гуманитарная помощь» | Delivery of military equipment to combat units |
| «безвозмездная передача войсковым частям» | Free transfer of matériel to units at the front, stated in a foundation's own accounts |
| «народный ОПК» | Nominally grassroots weapons manufacturing, convened and funded by the state |
| «закрытая платформа инженерного снабжения» | A military procurement marketplace, described so as to avoid saying so |
| «демонтажные работы» / «вахта по спецконтракту» | A one-year military contract with a brigade, advertised as construction work. The post that uses it (projectArchangel, 8–12.03.2026) names the 205th brigade and «Херсонское направление» in the same breath. Quote it, never adopt it |
| «спецоперация» / «СВО» | The invasion of Ukraine — retain in quotation when quoting, never adopt in our own prose |

Add to this table whenever a new sanitising term turns up.

### 5.2 Bold conventions

Bold a named individual or entity on **first mention** in a document; plain
thereafter. If an entity is bolded once in a section, bold it at every mention in
that section — never bold-then-plain for the same entity. Bolding used for
numeric emphasis is unaffected and does not have to carry through.

### 5.3 Open questions belong to the thing they are about

Unresolved items attach to the entity, person or product they concern, in the
registry, and the build renders the lists. Do not create a standing prose
catch-all list — the project has had one rot and be re-reported as current
before. Resolving something in the registry must strike it from every page at
once.

### 5.4 Adjacent-but-not-core entities

An entity with no confirmed ЦБСТ tie gets `tier: adjacent` and at most a
one-line disambiguation. It does not get a profile page and it does not enter the
narrative. Full detail stays in project memory.

### 5.5 Cross-linking

Every named entity that has a profile page is a link at its first mention on any
page. Every named individual links to their entry in the person index. From a RU
page, link to the RU version of the target.

### 5.6 Data-to-ink: every mark traces to a registry field

Every colour, shape, icon and line weight on a published page must name the `data/registry/*.json` field that produces it. A mark that cannot is decoration, and decoration must not imply severity, connection or scale. Structural chrome (a rule, a margin, a heading face) is allowed; say that it is chrome.

- Evidence-grade colours (`ok` / `mid` / `low`, the `{doc}` / `{press}` / `{open}` tags) are used for grades only and always carry the uppercase label. `accent` and `ink-3` are structural, never a finding.
- Node colour is the registry `tier`; node shape is the graph `kind`; a product glyph is the output of `classify_product_icon.py`; a hub badge is `classify_variant()`. If the underlying value changes, the mark changes with it.
- A number on a landing page is shown at its point of use (a chip on the case line, coloured by the sanction / anomaly / scale pattern it reports), not as a block of stat tiles.
- Hand-written prose is analysis and need not trace to a field. Do not present it as data.
- Retired 2026-10-02 from the Artifact «Data-to-Ink Rules» (local copy `data/sources/ARTIFACT_20260921_data-to-ink-rules.html`, s8953); its tier counts and glyph audit were out of date.
