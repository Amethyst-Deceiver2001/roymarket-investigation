Demand-Side Icon Set

The graph currently only draws the supply side — manufacturers, their products, their owners. This proposes glyphs for who's on the other end of a sale: a named military unit that received or fielded a product, a government body that procured it under contract, and a volunteer/procurement fund that bought it and delivered it to the front — three demand-side actor types, each grounded in a real, already-sourced field in the registry, not invented for symmetry with the supply side.

Two more demand-side categories were tested against the corpus and not built — see §03. That's a deliberate finding, the same discipline the Product Icon Set used to reject a speculative EO/IR-camera category: a shape nobody's data backs is worse than no shape at all.

Edge glyphs — how a demand-side node connects back to a product

Procures (government contract)
Product/entity → institutional buyer. Sourced from a real government_contracts[] entry (contract number, date, sometimes value) — an evidentiary edge, not an inference.
Confirmed field use
Product → military unit. Sourced from battlefield_evidence[] — a thank-you letter, an MoD-attributed kill claim, a named-unit testimonial. Weaker than a contract (self-reported/press, not a procurement record) — render thinner/dashed to keep that distinction visible.

Why a shield, not a circle: the existing tier palette (Manufacturer/ЦБСТ core/Plumbing/Money layer/Adjacent/Network/Unverified) already owns node color, and every current node — company or fund — renders as the same circle. Giving military units and institutional buyers their own base silhouette means a reader can tell "who makes it," "who bought it," and "who used it" apart at a glance, the same shape-carries-category move the Product Icon Set uses against the product square. Why the fund gets a variant, not a new base shape: it's already a node today (tier: Money layer) — this only proposes swapping its icon, no graph-schema change required, unlike the other two rows.

03 Tested, not built

The user's original ask also named "companies buying products and/or services (financial, training etc.)" — checked against the corpus the same way the EO/IR-camera and UGV hypotheses were checked in the Product Icon Set, before drawing anything:

Financial-services buyer (leasing / credit / investment)

9 entities contain financing-adjacent language (лизинг/кредит/инвестици/финансировани) somewhere in free text — but on inspection this is almost entirely the entity's own financing arrangements (a manufacturer describing its equipment leasing terms to end customers, or its own investment round), not a documented case of another company buying products as a financial-services transaction. The one clean counter-example — a procurement fund purchasing hardware and donating it forward — is already covered by the "volunteer/procurement fund" node above. No separate financial-buyer entity type survives contact with real text.

Training-provider buyer

15 entities contain training-adjacent language (обучение/курс/подготовка) — but every instance found is the manufacturer describing operator-training it offers (a value-add service on top of its own product), never a third-party training academy or school recorded as a customer relationship. state_programs[] (31 entries) looked like a candidate source but turned out to be almost entirely GISP/rk.2035.university registry-listing participation, not training or funding relationships.

Both would need a real structured field first — a training_delivered[] or financed_by[] array with its own source_id, built the same disciplined way government_contracts[] was — before either is icon-worthy. Building the glyph ahead of the data would be indistinguishable from guessing.

Mockup only — not wired into the live Network Graph artifact, and two of the three node types aren't graph nodes yet at all (§01). Financial-buyer and training-provider categories were tested against the corpus and deliberately not built (§03) — that's a finding, logged the same way the Product Icon Set logs its rejected EO/IR-camera hypothesis. Grounded in general node/glyph literature: shape (not hue alone) is the standard way to separate node kinds in heterogeneous/bipartite network drawings (Cambridge Intelligence, "Get more from your graphs with glyphs"; bipartite-network visualization survey work), and recognizable real-world icon metaphors read faster than abstract shapes in buyer/supplier network diagrams (yFiles/Creately supply-chain visualization guidance) — both consistent with this project's existing dataviz-skill rule that identity is never color-alone.
